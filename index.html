Dorf Nelson & Zauderer LLP

For a New York law firm, I rebuilt their website and built a private billing portal — one secure login where the owner sees the whole financial picture of the firm. It updates from their QuickBooks and Clio exports, so the numbers are always current.

Inside the billing portal
  • Overview — cash on hand, total owed to the firm, revenue and profit for the year, all on one screen
  • Who owes money — every unpaid bill sorted by how overdue it is (current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days)
  • Oldest unpaid bills — the top 25 most overdue accounts, so the firm knows who to chase first
  • By originating attorney — how much each attorney's clients owe, and how much of that is overdue
  • Profit & loss — income, expenses, profit margin, and biggest expense categories
  • Partner distributions — what each partner has drawn for the year and their share of the pool
  • Cash & balance sheet — bank balances, assets, and what the firm owes
  • Bills to pay — vendor bills owed by the firm, sorted by how overdue they are
  • Work in progress — time worked but not yet billed (hours and dollar value)
  • Top clients — biggest clients by revenue and their share of the book
The origination calculator
  • Reads each payment that comes in and splits it across the right attorneys using the firm's own commission rules
  • Spits out a clean Excel workbook — one tab per billing attorney — ready to hand off
Also built
  • Upload & auto-update — drop in the QuickBooks/Clio reports and the portal refreshes itself, with plain-English insights written up after each upload
  • Rebuilt the public website — attorney pages, practice areas, news
  • Got the firm to show up in AI search — when people ask ChatGPT, Google AI, or Perplexity for a top litigator, the firm comes up
  • Everything is encrypted and owner-only — data unlocks in the browser after login

All Sportz Apparel

For a custom team-uniform manufacturer, I rebuilt their website and built a private dashboard that turns their numbers into live charts the owner can read at a glance.

  • Revenue charts — money in by month (actual vs. forecast) and a 13-week revenue projection
  • Production charts — forecast vs. actual output each week and by customer, plus factory capacity used vs. the target
  • Money-owed charts — invoices per day, expected payments by due date, and a list of who owes what and how overdue it is
  • Shipping & inventory charts — what ships each day by customer, the delivery schedule, and stock on hand (raw fabric, in-progress, finished)
  • An employee scoreboard and a built-in assistant that answers plain-English questions about the business
